Halo Sky · Gram · Fingerprints

Methods

Where the numbers behind the Halo Sky Gram and Fingerprints come from, and what they can and cannot show.

The model data

All cloud and temperature values come from ECMWF's Integrated Forecasting System (IFS), delivered by Open-Meteo under CC BY 4.0. They are model values, not observations. The record of past hours is Open-Meteo's archive of the first hours of each forecast run, joined into a continuous series.

The heights of the pressure levels are worked out from the temperatures and the surface pressure (the hypsometric equation). Hours are counted as daylight when the sun is at least 0° above the horizon, and as civil twilight down to −6°. In Fingerprints the cloud cover is rounded to 10 % steps.

Low, mid and high cloud

These are ECMWF's own cloud fields, made by the model's cloud and convection schemes. ECMWF splits them by pressure relative to the surface: low cloud lies below 0.8 of the surface pressure, mid cloud between 0.8 and 0.45, and high cloud above 0.45. At sea level that is about 800 and 450 hPa, roughly 2 and 6 km.

The split is by pressure, not by temperature. At high-altitude sites the mid band reaches cirrus heights, which is why Fingerprints hides them in the mid-band search unless asked. At continental sites in summer the mid/high boundary can be as warm as about −17 °C (as at Hotan and Tbilisi), so some of the band called mid is ice cloud.

The bands count cloud that the model puts in only part of its grid box, such as cumulus, even when the box as a whole is far from saturated.

Cloud on the height levels comes from humidity

The 0.25° pressure-level data carry no cloud fraction of their own. Open-Meteo works the cloud cover out from the relative humidity on each level with the Sundqvist method. Comparing the two fields shows the relation exactly, the same on every level:

Relative humidityCloud cover
70 % or lessnone
80 %18 %
90 %42 %
100 %full

These values fit the Sundqvist formula with a critical humidity of 70 %:

cover = 1 − √((1 − RH) / (1 − 0.70)), for RH above 70 %

ECMWF gives this humidity relative to liquid water above 0 °C and to ice below −23 °C, with a smooth blend in between. Three things follow for halo watching:

  • The height gram shows layers that are close to saturation, which is what sheets of cirrus and altocumulus usually are.
  • The humidity stops at 100 %, so the ice supersaturation in which cirrus forms cannot be seen.
  • With only 13 levels, a thin layer between two levels can be missed or spread out. The Smooth style blends between levels; the Pressure-level blocks style shows each level's own value.

Why the two grams can disagree

The low/mid/high bands and the height gram show two different kinds of cloud. The bands are the model's own cloud, including partial cloud such as cumulus. The height gram is humidity turned into cloud, and below 70 % humidity it shows nothing. So the bands can show cloud where the height gram is empty, most often with convective cloud in the tropics and summer.

An example: in the forecast for Puerto La Cruz, Venezuela, for 18–20 November 2026, ECMWF's mid band reaches about 20 % every afternoon around 14:00, apparently the tops of the day's cumulus. The relative humidity peaks at only 68 % at 700 hPa, 48 % at 600 hPa and 11 % at 500 hPa, so the height gram shows no mid cloud at all. Low cloud is similar: the band reaches 53 %, while the humidity at 925 and 850 hPa stays between 69 and 80 %, which gives only a faint trace.

Halo-making cloud usually comes in near-saturated layers, while cumulus mostly is not halo material. Reading the two grams together helps to tell the two kinds of cloud apart.

ECMWF model upgrades

ECMWF upgrades the IFS about once a year. Two upgrades have left a clear mark in the 9 km cloud bands. We compared each calendar month with the same month in other years at 21 sites around the world, from Tampere to Lima, and looked for changes that most sites share at the same moment:

DateIFS cycleWhat changed in the bandsSites
12 Oct 202147r3, new cloud and convection physicsHours with no mid cloud at all: 5 points more of all hours. Average high cloud cover: 3 points more.18 of 21
12 May 202650r1More hours of faint cloud (1–9 %): low 3 points, mid 4 points, high 2 points more of all hours.17–19 of 21

Open-Meteo began taking ECMWF's own bands for the 0.25° model around April 2025, having computed them from humidity before. That change does not touch the 9 km bands used here, and they show no break at that time.

What the upgrades mean:

  • Fingerprints pool all the years since 2017, so their percentages blend model versions. Comparisons between sites stay fair, because every site has the same blend.
  • Single years can show steps that are not weather. The year-by-year view in Seasons marks the two upgrades in red, and years on either side of a mark are not fully comparable.
  • The faint cloud added in 2026 matters little so far: it covers a few months of almost ten years, and cover under 5 % rounds to zero in the 10 % steps of Fingerprints.
